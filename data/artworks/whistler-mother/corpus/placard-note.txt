Arrangement in Grey and Black. The mother sits in strict profile, still as a coin, hands folded over a white handkerchief, feet resting on a low stool. The wall behind her is a field of measured grey; a curtain falls at the left edge, flecked like a night sky; a framed etching of the Thames hangs high and silent. Black dress, lace cap, lace cuffs — the only whites in a picture built from a handful of tones. Whistler laid the paint thin, almost staining it into the canvas, weighing every interval of wall against every interval of figure. He asked that the picture be seen as an arrangement before it is seen as a mother; the public saw the mother anyway, and both readings have hung together on this wall ever since. Painted in Chelsea in 1871, shown at the Royal Academy in 1872, bought by France in 1891 — the first American painting to enter the national collection, argued into the Luxembourg by Mallarmé and Duret, passed through the Louvre, and settled at last in the old railway station on the left bank of the Seine, the Musée d'Orsay, where the greys still hold their balance and the profile still refuses to turn.
