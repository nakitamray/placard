Divide the colour and the light recomposes. The divided touch does not mix its pigments on the palette; it sets pure hues side by side in small separate strokes, and trusts the eye of the beholder to fuse them into a shimmer no mixed pigment can reach. Contrast of tone, contrast of hue, contrast of line: a colour is brightest beside its complementary, orange against blue, and a line carries feeling by its direction — ascending for joy, level for calm, descending for sadness. The painter measures, the canvas vibrates. Optical mixture, simultaneous contrast, the calculated harmony of tint and tone and line: this was the doctrine of the neo-impressionists, chromo-luminarism, pointillism to its critics, and Georges Seurat carried it with the patience of a geometer, dot by dot by dot, from the bathers at Asnières to the Grande Jatte to the ring of the circus, where the method built by measurement was asked, at the last, to paint delight itself.
